# Staged construction review

Use for assets assembled or revealed in stages, such as buildings, machines, vehicles, and characters built from parts. Review each asset’s sequence through its completed state on the first animatable assembly and again on the finished export. A shared animation or rig still needs checking with each supported asset whose geometry or timing differs.

1. **List the actual stages and changes.** Start with the approved 2D stage art and compare it with the animation timeline, visibility changes, or runtime build states. Identify what appears, moves, connects, or leaves, from the first visible stage to the final asset. Include short-lived construction tools and supports and any interruptions the game actually shows.
2. **Account for every visible piece.** Identify what each piece does or communicates at that moment. A piece can become part of the asset, support or guide construction, or provide a deliberate progress cue, ornament, or effect. For a physical part, trace where it comes from, what moves or supports it, where it seats, and where it goes if temporary. Check that the action and order suit the game's presentation. Investigate unexplained pieces and motion, then remove, reposition, or give them a clear role within the approved design.
3. **Review each stage in isolation.** Capture consistent views of every stage, with close or alternate views when joinery or supports are hidden. Run all applicable `review-game-art` checks at each stage. Compare the stage with its approved art for silhouette, style, materials, and appropriate detail. Check alignment and intended symmetry against the actual centerline or repeat pattern, including exposed surfaces and temporary pieces. Verify support, contact, fit, continuity, and a clear read from the game camera. A deliberately one-sided phase can work when it follows the approved order and reads as work in progress.
4. **Review every transition in motion.** Play the entire sequence at normal speed, then step through each change where parts enter, pass close to another part, attach, or leave. Check the full travel for clashes, unsupported spans, misaligned seating, flicker, and unexplained pops or disappearances. Confirm that materials, placement, and attachments agree at the handoff to the completed asset. Review the runtime sequence when timing or visibility is driven by the game.
5. **Keep the evidence tied to the asset and stage.** A labeled contact sheet and a preview of the full sequence are often enough to orient the review. Track each asset and stage or transition by name, frame, or time. Record the relevant capture, verdict, and any fix. For example, if a roof panel arrives while an unused guide floats in the shot and clips a brace, check the guide's role, path, and removal before approving that transition. Recheck the affected stage and its adjacent transitions after the fix.
