# Material and texture review

Use this check when designing, texturing, or reviewing an asset whose surfaces are part of the approved look. Review the first representative material application before repeating it across an asset family. Review the actual exported textures and materials again at handoff.

- **Identify the surface decisions in the concept.** Mark the important material regions and the details that define them. Note the intended colors and value relationships, finish, markings, seams, wear, and the scale and placement of visible details. For staged assets, agree which surfaces are exposed or finished at each stage. Use the approved art and documented materials references to resolve ambiguous details.
- **Compare matching views.** Put the approved image and current asset side by side at comparable angles and scale. Review at play size and up close when that view is supported. Use neutral lighting to diagnose the materials and the intended game lighting to check the result. Identify missing or simplified features by surface and location. A surface should carry the concept's characteristic forms, detail, and material differences at the views where they matter.
- **Inspect the maps and the lit result.** Check the maps the asset actually uses. Inspect color and markings for missing regions, baked light where it conflicts with the intended finish, and distorted patterns. Where used, check that roughness, metalness, normal, height, and emissive maps give the intended surface response and reinforce real features. Look for flat or uniform treatment where the concept shows distinct finishes, generic noise where the concept shows deliberate surface detail, and seams, stretching, or inconsistent texture detail across adjoining areas. Relight or rotate the asset to verify maps work together, then check the runtime export for detail lost to import, compression, or distance.
- **Check transparency and cutouts in motion.** Move the gameplay camera around and away from foliage, hair cards, grates, glass, or similar surfaces through the supported viewing range. Look for sorting pops, visible card intersections, edge halos, or silhouettes that thin out or disappear prematurely at distance. Inspect overlapping layers against representative backgrounds in the runtime export.
- **Add the right detail.** Establish the large material and value groups first. Develop visible seams, panel or fabric detail, markings, grain, and use-related wear where the concept calls for them. Keep simpler surfaces where they support the style and hierarchy. For example, if a concept shows painted cladding, scuffed edges, and bare fittings with distinct finishes, inspect those three regions separately. Restore the missing separation and surface detail before repeating that material treatment elsewhere.

Record the concept view, runtime view, and the source of any mismatch. Recheck the changed maps and the exported asset. Revisit concept approval if a substantial change in the approved look is proposed.
