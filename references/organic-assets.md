# Organic anatomy and movement

Look for a subject close to the intended species or body type, at a similar speed and performing the same action. A skeleton or anatomy diagram establishes attachment points and joint orientation. Footage or sequential photographs show timing, weight shift, and how the whole body participates. For an invented creature, document which real analogues informed its body and how its own anatomy works in the approved concept.

## Review the anatomy and motion

- **Trace the body through the pose.** Use consistent front, side, and other necessary views. Trace a hand, paw, wing, or tail through its joints to its attachment on the body, including where it passes behind fur, clothing, or another limb. Confirm the number and orientation of limbs and digits and the placement of major landmarks. Keep deliberate asymmetry and deformation consistent with the concept and the action.
- **Follow the joint and the surrounding tissue.** Compare the bend, twist, and range with reference for that anatomy. Check that motion is shared across the relevant joints. When a human turns a palm, follow the rotation through the forearm and check that the hand stays seated at the wrist. During wing folding, follow the attached wing from the shoulder into its segments and check how the feathers overlap. In plants or soft-bodied creatures, follow how bends travel through the connected form and how leaves, branches, or appendages follow it.
- **Check the face and breathing.** In visible expressions, blinks, vocalizations, and breaths, check that the jaw moves from its intended joint, the eyes stay seated as they turn, and the eyelids follow their surfaces. Check that the chest, gills, or other visible breathing structures move in a way that fits the anatomy. Look for deformation that breaks the underlying skull or body structure.
- **Check contact and transfer of weight.** At contact, loading, push-off, and release, follow the foot or paw against the ground and the body over its supports. Look for planted contacts that slide or sink, paws that pop between frames, and landings with no visible response in the body. Check footfall order against the intended gait. For reaching, carrying, or climbing, follow where the hand grips and how the rest of the body shifts around that contact. For flight or swimming, compare how the stroke and body travel relate in the chosen reference.
- **Check anchors and flexibility.** For rooted plants and anchored creatures, track the base against its support during a sway, lunge, or recovery. Check for unintended sliding, lifting, or rotation. Match how the bend and stiffness vary along trunks, stems, and tips to the anatomy and movement reference.
- **Check follow-through.** Watch how tails, ears, feathers, soft tissue, and foliage respond when the main body accelerates, stops, or changes direction. Check that they follow and settle with timing that fits their weight and flexibility, and that the motion stays continuous at loop seams and transitions.
- **Check the rig and deformation in 3D.** Inspect the posed mesh with its skeleton visible from more than one angle. Compare joint pivots, bend directions, hierarchy, and limits with the design. If a part drifts, pops, or disconnects, check its parent and skin weights or attachment. Inspect both the compressed and stretched sides of the joint. Look for pinching, corkscrew twists, unexpected loss of volume, mesh cracks, and clothing or parts that cut through each other where the problem is visible. Recheck the exported motion on the game rig through the action and relevant transitions.
- **Check frame continuity in 2D.** Overlay neighboring frames aligned to the intended registration point and flip through the sequence. Follow landmarks, limb lengths, digits, and contacts through occlusion. Check that parts reappear on the correct side, stay attached, and keep their identity. Play the complete loop in the game or preview to catch jumps in pose, contact, or body position at the seam.
- **Exercise combined movements in-game.** Check the combinations the player can see, such as turning while reaching, changing gait on a slope, or performing an action while moving. Review the blend or transition from the gameplay camera and inspect problem frames up close. Check that planted contacts hold, limbs stay attached, and clearances hold through the combined action.

## Observed and illustrated references

- **Human joints and range of motion.** Figures 9.12 and 9.13 illustrate movement of the shoulder, hip, forearm, wrist, ankle, and other joints. Use the drawings to find which part contributes to a bend or twist. [OpenStax, *Types of Body Movements*](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-5-types-of-body-movements)
- **Human weight transfer and carrying.** Step through the photographs and track foot placement and the carrying hand while the subject descends stairs. [Eadweard Muybridge, *Animal Locomotion*, plate 143 (1887), National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/181203-plate-number-143-descending-stairs-cup-and-saucer-right-hand)
- **Quadruped gait.** Compare the same dog's trot and gallop. Mark each paw touchdown and lift-off, then track the torso between those frames. [Muybridge, mastiff trotting, plate 705 (1887)](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/221203-plate-number-705-dog-trotting-mastiff-dread), [mastiff galloping, plate 707 (1887)](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/167096-plate-number-707-dread-galloping)
- **Bird wings and feet.** Study the folded wing and the top and underside drawings. Follow the skeleton and compare how the feathers overlap. Use the feet diagrams to check toe attachment and orientation for the intended kind of foot. [Cornell Lab of Ornithology, *Bird Anatomy*](https://www.birds.cornell.edu/home/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/LCF-Bird-Anatomy-Cheat-Sheet.pdf)
- **Plant movement.** Follow a fern frond from base to tip during unfurling and use the footage to study the order of shape changes. [Darren Wilch, fern unfurling time-lapse (2020)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z5iK8D-5T8s)
